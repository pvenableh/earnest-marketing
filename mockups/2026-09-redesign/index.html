Earnest. Landing page redesign · three concepts · September 2026

Three ways to sell the same true thing.

Earnest is an organization with a voice: a real language model grounded in a studio’s own clients, work, money, marketing and schedule, that writes in the studio’s brand and then does the work, with nothing reaching a client or moving money without a tap. Each concept below leads with a different one of those claims, and earns the rest on the way down. Every mockup is built in one of the app’s own three looks, so the site and the product match. Every number in them is from the live demo workspace.

The organization LLMFocus, the Context Broker, AI Actions. It knows the business, not a blank prompt.
Brand awarenessDirection, audience, voice set once, per org and per client, read by every draft and document.
ExecutionTasks, projects, clients, money, marketing, schedules, run in six apps on one rail.
The guardrailNothing reaches a client or moves money without your tap. It is the reason a studio can trust the first three.

Concept A

It already knows.

Lead with the model. The hero is Focus itself, working on sample data: four questions, four grounded answers, each one showing what Earnest read before it spoke. The rest of the page explains why it could answer.

  • Differentiates on the one thing HoneyBook, Bonsai and a ChatGPT tab cannot claim: an LLM that starts from the studio’s live data.
  • The Context Broker diagram makes “organization LLM” concrete: six sources, one door, three kinds of output.
  • Brand is shown as cause and effect: one profile card, three drafts that sound like it.
  • AI Actions is a proposed change with a held email, so the guardrail is demonstrated, not asserted.
Sells the LLM
Hero demo, Context Broker section, FAQ
Sells brand
Profile → proposal, post, reminder
Sells execution
AI Actions diff, six-app strip, Decide · Do · Know
Best for
Buyers who have tried generic AI and found it hollow; the current positioning

Risk. The breadth of the product is a strip, not a section. A visitor shopping for “a CRM with invoicing” has to scroll to find it. Mitigated by the six-app strip and the nav.

Concept B

One place.

Lead with breadth. The hero is an app switcher: six tabs, six real screens, and an Earnest chip that changes with the room. It says, in one device, that the studio runs here and that the model is in every part of it.

  • Answers the first question a studio owner has: does it do everything I do? Six real screens say yes before any copy does.
  • The “what it replaces” strip prices the switch: nine tools out, one in.
  • The bento is execution-first, with demo numbers on the tiles rather than adjectives.
  • The LLM gets its own section, “Earnest is in every room”, with three grounded answers from three apps.
Sells the LLM
The chip in every tab, then the “every room” section
Sells brand
A filled-in brand form beside four outputs, including documents
Sells execution
The hero itself, the bento, the capacity ladder
Best for
SEO landings and comparison shoppers; studios consolidating tools

Risk. Breadth-first pages read as “another all-in-one”, which is a crowded shelf. The chip in every tab and the “every room” section have to carry the difference, and they sit below the switcher.

Concept C

A day with it.

Lead with the outcome. The page is one Tuesday, 8:07 to 6:00, eight stops, each one an app and a thing Earnest drafted and a thing you tapped. Breadth, the model and the brand all arrive as plot rather than as features.

  • The most memorable of the three and the easiest to retell: “it drafted the reminders, I sent five and held one.”
  • Every stop shows the division of labour explicitly, Earnest / You, so the guardrail is the structure of the page.
  • The tally (7 decisions, 17 drafts, 0 sent without you) turns the safety promise into a number.
  • Brand is proven in retrospect: it wrote in your voice all day because you told it once.
Sells the LLM
Every stop; the 6:00 Mirror stop names Focus
Sells brand
Voice at 9:30, 10:45, 2:00, 3:30; the brand section after the tally
Sells execution
Eight stops across six apps; the “everything it touched” grid
Best for
Founder-led selling, referrals, the blog’s audience, launch posts

Risk. Long. A skimmer wants the feature list this page refuses to lead with, and the story only works if the screenshots keep matching the copy, so it costs the most to keep true.

Recommendation

Ship A as the homepage, with B’s app switcher as its breadth section. The grounded-LLM claim is the one competitors cannot copy by adding a feature, and the Focus demo is the fastest way to prove it: a visitor asks, it answers from real data, and the “Read” strip shows why. A’s only weakness is breadth, and B’s switcher fixes it in one section without a paragraph.

  1. Keep C. It is the blog post, the launch email and the sales deck. Its eight stops are also the outline for a product tour inside the demo.
  2. Use B as the shape of the per-feature pages and any SEO landing (“CRM for design studios”), where the visitor arrives with a category in mind.
  3. Whichever ships, keep the guardrail in the hero’s fine print, as every concept does. It is the sentence that lets a studio owner trust the rest.
